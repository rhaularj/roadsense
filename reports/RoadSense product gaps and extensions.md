# Turn RoadSense detections into city work orders

RoadSense is a working laptop-scale prototype. It takes dashcam video plus a GPS log, finds road damage on the device, blurs the frame, projects each defect onto the map, and passes it through guarded LLM agents that verify, deduplicate, triage and route it. A driver approves each report, locked by a hash, before it is filed. The one place it can win is the **verified-to-ticket loop**. None of the surveyed competitors (Vialytics, Cyvl, RoadAI, Blyncsy, Samsara, Waymo/Waze, Nexar) advertises filing deduplicated, human-approved defects into a city's 311 or work-order system. They stop at a map, a dashboard or a condition score. Everything past "approved" is a demo today. "Halifax 311" is a local mock Open311 server. HRM does not appear to publish an Open311 endpoint, and its real system of record is Cityworks. The province gets a JSON file written to disk. The system has one shared token, no user identity, in-memory jobs, SQLite, and default use of non-Canadian free LLM endpoints, which Nova Scotia's data-residency law likely forbids for municipal use. About **29% of the reports it creates are still false alarms** (report precision about 0.71), and it rejects only 17-20% of false alarms in agent evaluations. The extensions that matter most are, in order: make delivery real and Canadian-hosted (Cityworks/ArcGIS adapters, signed media, retention, identity), fix the cheap decision-layer accuracy bugs and collect Halifax ground truth, then mount the pipeline on city fleets that repeat routes and add segment-level condition scoring. Free fleet and connected-vehicle data from Samsara, Waze and Nexar is about to make "selling detections" worthless. Verification, triage, routing and integration are the defensible layer.

## A guarded agent pipeline that stops one hop short of the city

The pipeline is real end to end on one machine. On the edge, video is sampled at 5 fps through YOLOv8m with BoT-SORT tracking. Each physical defect becomes one event, with its best frame chosen by confidence × sharpness. Everything outside the damage box is downsampled and Gaussian-blurred. A flat-road pinhole model places the defect, with distances over 40 m untrusted ([process_video.py](../roadsense/edge/process_video.py), [tracker.py](../roadsense/edge/tracker.py), [redact.py](../roadsense/edge/redact.py), [camera.py](../roadsense/edge/camera.py)). Events travel through a store-and-forward outbox to a FastAPI agent API. The API snaps them to the road with OSRM and hands them to an OpenRouter orchestrator, which delegates to five specialists (vision, location, triage, filing, follow-up). Each specialist works over four stdio MCP servers, under a tool allow-list and a pydantic output contract ([agent_api.py](../roadsense/services/agent_api.py), [orchestrator.py](../roadsense/agents/orchestrator.py), [specialists.py](../roadsense/agents/specialists.py)).

The guarded decision layer is the defensible engineering. A verdict guard refuses "confirmed" or "disputed" without verifier evidence. Dedupe merges same-class sightings within 15 m and counts distinct trips as confirmations. Severity flags potholes ≥25 cm wide as HRM Priority 1 candidates. Jurisdiction sends 100-series highways to Nova Scotia Public Works ([reports.py](../roadsense/core/reports.py), [geo.py](../roadsense/core/geo.py)). Approval builds the payload deterministically from the database, stores the SHA-256 the driver saw, and re-hashes before sending, so the agent cannot change a report after a human signs off ([open311.py](../roadsense/core/open311.py)). A deterministic fallback finishes any event the agents skip. In practice the LLM is mostly a narrator over deterministic tools. That makes the system robust, and it also means the "agentic" label carries less of the value than the rules do.

The README is candid about what is mocked: the test videos (RDD2022 stills from China, Japan and the US, placed at Halifax coordinates), an offline fixture of two roads, the public OSRM demo server, the Open311 mock, the provincial referral JSON, and uncalibrated camera defaults ([README.md](../README.md), [demo/trip/manifest.json](../demo/trip/manifest.json)). The authority directory has exactly two entries. The service codes `POTHOLE` and `ROAD_SURFACE` were invented for the mock. The `media_url` a city would receive points at `127.0.0.1:8000` ([municipalities.json](../roadsense/core/municipalities.json), [mock_open311.py](../roadsense/services/mock_open311.py), [config.py](../roadsense/common/config.py)). Nova Scotia rules are hard-coded in Python constants and prompt text. Moving to another city therefore means changing code, not configuration.

## Halifax's real stack is Cityworks, not Open311

The most consequential product gap is the integration target. HRM takes pothole reports by phone and web form through 311. It records them in Cityworks and publishes **Cityworks Service Requests and Work Orders** on its ArcGIS Hub open-data portal, through ArcGIS REST and GeoJSON under the Open Government Licence ([HRM Open Data](https://data-hrm.hub.arcgis.com/pages/notifications-feedback), [HRM Open Data FAQ](https://data-hrm.hub.arcgis.com/pages/open-data-common-questions)). The research found no Halifax Open311 endpoint. Provincial roads and 100-series highways go to 511 or the NS Public Works Operations Contact Centre, not 311 ([HRM: Report a pothole](https://www.halifax.ca/transportation/streets-sidewalks/paving-repair/report-a-pothole)). This is good news in disguise. RoadSense can read HRM's open Cityworks data **today, without write access**, to dedupe against existing open requests and to track closures. That would be a low-risk first integration that turns the "follow-up" agent from a mock poll into a real feedback loop.

Even where Open311 exists, it is a thin intake standard. GeoReport v2 allows a single `media_url` with no upload convention, a binary open/closed status, and a 4,000-character description ([Open311 GeoReport v2](https://wiki.open311.org/GeoReport_v2)). That cannot carry multi-frame evidence, dimensions or severity bands. The typical Canadian pattern is a CRM intake layer (Salesforce in Toronto, CivicPlus/SeeClickFix elsewhere) dispatching to Cityworks on Esri ([Toronto staff report](https://www.toronto.ca/legdocs/mmis/2023/gg/bgrd/backgroundfile-238098.pdf)). Connectors into Cityworks need a Citizen Engagement API licence and an "API Only" integration user ([CivicPlus Cityworks connector](https://www.civicplus.help/seeclickfix/docs/seeclickfix-connect-for-cityworks-requirements-configuration)). RoadSense should keep its Open311 adapter and add Cityworks REST and ArcGIS Feature Service adapters beside it.

The buyer may also be different from the one the MVP assumes. In Barrie, Ontario, **about 90% of potholes are found by Roads Department operators** and only about 7% by citizens. Each repair is tied to a road segment and a timestamp in Cityworks ([Esri Canada](https://resources.esri.ca/cityworks/integrating-arcgis-and-cityworks-for-a-complete-pothole-management-solution)). If Halifax looks similar, RoadSense's natural role is a **road-patrol and inspection tool that feeds work orders** for Roads Operations, not a citizen 311 channel. The output is then an inspection finding or work order, and the integration and the sales pitch change with it.

Delivery robustness has its own bugs. If submission fails, the report stays `approved` and has no resend path. A later merge into an approved, unsent report changes its confirmations, so the hash no longer matches and sending is refused permanently. Merges into reports that were already submitted are never pushed to the city. Follow-up has no scheduler ([municipality_server.py](../roadsense/mcp_servers/municipality_server.py), [reports.py](../roadsense/core/reports.py)).

## Accuracy is unproven in Halifax, and the cheapest fixes are in the rules

Model accuracy has a ceiling. On the 350-image verifier evaluation, the RT-DETR second opinion raised precision only from **0.714 to 0.744**. It cut false alarms by 21% and kept 92% of true damage, and edge recall was only 0.641 ([results/verifier_eval.json](../results/verifier_eval.json)). In the deterministic agent evaluation over 42 scenarios, report precision was 0.706 with 2 of 12 false alarms rejected. The single LLM run over 17 scenarios rejected 1 of 5, took 214 s per trip and used about 102k tokens ([results/agent_eval.json](../results/agent_eval.json), [results/agent_eval_llm.json](../results/agent_eval_llm.json)). The headline 100% jurisdiction and dedupe scores are scored against the same two-segment fixture that generates the test points. Duplicate scenarios reuse the same image offset by about 4 m, and no test places two distinct potholes within 15 m ([eval_agents.py](../scripts/eval_agents.py)). Real-world precision, recall and position accuracy in Halifax are unknown, because no labelled local footage exists.

The field data suggests a better architecture will not fix this. Road-damage detection on RDD2022 has levelled off at about **0.70-0.79 F1 for challenge-grade ensembles** ([CRDDC-2022 overview](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.11362v1), [ORDDC'2024 entry](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.08409v1)). One CRDDC entry scored 81.7% F1 on US data but 74.1% across countries, a domain-shift gap larger than the 2-3 mAP gains claimed for YOLO11 variants ([arXiv 2211.00091](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.00091v1), [PMC12469154](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12469154/)). No public Canadian dataset labels damage in snow or at night. Montreal's ViSnow has about 294,000 images labelled for snow cover only ([IEEE DataPort ViSnow](https://ieee-dataport.org/node/12819)). General VLMs are mediocre zero-shot judges: GPT-4V scores 74.5% on RoadBench against 78.6% for the fine-tuned RoadCLIP ([RoadBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.17353v1)). That supports RoadSense's choice to use VLMs as verifiers rather than primary detectors. Local Halifax winter footage, labelled partly for free from drivers' approve and reject clicks, will move accuracy more than any model swap.

Several accuracy losses come from decision-layer bugs that can be fixed in days:

- **Dedupe requires the same class.** With 12% class disputes, one pothole seen as D40 and as D20 becomes two reports.
- **Rejected reports reappear.** Rejected and fixed reports are excluded from dedupe, so a pothole the driver rejected is proposed again on every trip.
- **Jurisdiction is a free string.** The agent can write any jurisdiction, and a bad one fails only at send time.
- **The agent controls severity.** The agent's severity overrides the rule severity, so the LLM can raise or lower priority.
- **The LLM cache can replay the wrong events.** The cache key strips event ids, so two similar trips can share one cached answer.
- **Evidence can be overwritten.** A re-upload with the same event id overwrites the stored image before the duplicate check runs.

([reports.py](../roadsense/core/reports.py), [contracts.py](../roadsense/agents/contracts.py), [reports_server.py](../roadsense/mcp_servers/reports_server.py), [llm.py](../roadsense/agents/llm.py), [agent_api.py](../roadsense/services/agent_api.py)). Severity is also weak at its root. Depth is never measured, and Priority 1 rests on an uncalibrated flat-road width estimate. Monocular depth models such as a distilled Depth Anything V2 (86 MB instead of 4.4 GB) give pothole area and *relative* depth, but not reliable metric depth ([arXiv 2505.21049](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.21049), [GLBIO 2026 abstract](https://d2j16w31g89z0j.cloudfront.net/site/2026w/abs/0680-GLBIO.pdf)). Severity should be reported as a band, with accelerometer "jolt" as corroboration, not as a measured depth.

## Privacy, liability and procurement set the real launch bar

Regulation is the hardest blocker. Nova Scotia's PIIDPA bars public bodies, including municipalities, from storing or accessing personal information outside Canada ([OIPC NS](https://oipc.novascotia.ca/sites/default/files/publications/FINAL%20-%20MGA%20PIA%20OIPC%202019%2011%2029_0.pdf)). Dashcam frames carry faces and plates. A new FOIPOP Act received Royal Assent on Oct 3, 2025 and takes effect **April 1, 2027**. It repeals PIIDPA but keeps cross-border restrictions "in accordance with regulations" that the research could not find, and it adds mandatory breach notification ([NS news release](https://news.novascotia.ca/en/2025/09/26/province-introduces-modernized-access-privacy-legislation), [NS Statutes 2025 c.13](https://nslegislature.ca/sites/default/files/legc/PDFs/annual%20statutes/2025%20Fall/c013.pdf)). RoadSense's defaults conflict with both laws. The LLM defaults are free OpenRouter models that "may log prompts". The SECURITY doc itself says a PIA and a Canadian no-retention LLM provider are needed before any pilot ([SECURITY.md](../SECURITY.md)).

The SaaS web path also breaks the documented privacy promises. SECURITY.md says raw video never leaves the device, yet the web upload stores the full video and GPS track server-side indefinitely. Each event also carries car position and timestamp, enough to rebuild a partial trajectory ([web/app.py](../roadsense/web/app.py), [schemas.py](../roadsense/common/schemas.py)). Redaction has no face or plate detector, leaves the damage box plus 15% unblurred, and is never checked on the server. Meanwhile `/media` serves images without authentication ([redact.py](../roadsense/edge/redact.py), [agent_api.py](../roadsense/services/agent_api.py)). The fix is known. EgoBlur (Apache 2.0) reports face AP 0.99 and recall 0.998, but it costs about 0.5 s per 2 MP frame on a GPU. It fits as a server-side second pass over the few stored crops, behind a lightweight on-device blur ([EgoBlur docs](https://facebookresearch.github.io/projectaria_tools/docs/open_models/egoblur), [EgoBlur paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.13093v2.pdf)).

Liability turns on notice. Nova Scotia pays pothole claims only when it had prior notice and missed its own service standard: in 2022 it received 935 claims and paid 105 ([CBC](https://amp.cbc.ca/news/canada/nova-scotia/most-nova-scotia-pothole-compensation-claims-get-denied-1.7148599)). Ontario codifies size, depth and day thresholds by road class in O. Reg. 239/02 ([e-Laws](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/020239)). A flood of AI detections could therefore start a city's legal clock on thousands of defects. This is the strongest argument *for* RoadSense's human-approval step and audit trail. Detections should arrive as triaged inspection findings mapped to statutory or HRM priority thresholds, not as auto-filed citizen complaints. The same logic argues against an "auto-send" mode, whatever drivers prefer.

On the operational side, the system cannot pass a security review. It has one shared bearer token and hard-codes the approver as `"driver"`. Most read endpoints and the whole web UI are unauthenticated, while Docker binds 0.0.0.0. It has no CI, its dependencies are unpinned despite claims that they are pinned, and it has no metrics or tracing ([agent_api.py](../roadsense/services/agent_api.py), [Dockerfile](../Dockerfile), [requirements.txt](../requirements.txt)). HRM buys under Administrative Order 2022-012-ADM, aligned with the NS Public Procurement Act and trade agreements, through competitive RFPs above low-value thresholds ([HRM Procurement](https://www.halifax.ca/business/doing-business-halifax/procurement)). Canadian AI road pilots usually run one season on one or two vehicles: Richmond Hill ran 135 days and fixed 542 potholes, and Gatineau used one truck ([Govlaunch Richmond Hill](https://govlaunch.com/governments/richmond-hill-on/projects/richmond-hill-on-improves-pothole-identification-and-repair-time-with-rover-ai-technology), [QCNA](https://qcna.qc.ca/gatineau-turns-to-artificial-intelligence-in-municipal-modernization/)). The RFP that follows will likely expect Canadian residency, a PIA, SSO, WCAG 2.1 AA (the NS ICT accessibility standard is still in development), and GIS export ([NS accessibility plan](https://novascotia.ca/accessibility/plan/government-accessibility-plan.pdf)). That last expectation is an inference from typical requirements, not a published HRM checklist.

## Free fleet data is commoditising detection, and Atlantic Canada is open

The market has split. Survey vendors sell periodic network condition ratings for capital planning: Vialytics, RoadAI (Montreal and Waterloo), Cyvl, Blyncsy/Bentley and Pavemetrics-based survey vans ([Xweather Montreal](https://www.xweather.com/blog/article/roadai-x-montreal), [Business Wire: Cyvl](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251001717298/en)). Fleet and data players have pushed real-time pothole feeds since 2026. Samsara's Ground Intelligence (May 2026) maps potholes from trucks already on the road and estimates deterioration ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/12/potholes-cost-cities-millions-samsara-using-ai-trucks-fix/)). Waymo shares pothole detections through the **free** Waze for Cities platform ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/09/waymo-robotaxis-are-tracking-potholes-and-sharing-that-data-with-waze-users)). Nexar claims 700k+ vehicles with API and Esri delivery ([GPS World](https://www.gpsworld.com/nexar-releases-citystream-live-a-real-time-mapping-platform-for-mobility/)). If detections become free, RoadSense should not compete on detection. It should become the **aggregator**: ingest its own dashcam events, third-party feeds and citizen 311 requests, dedupe across all of them, and file one verified ticket.

Prices give a usable anchor. Vialytics' public small-municipality contracts run about **US$4.3k-14.6k per year plus a setup fee**, for example $4,320 plus $475 in Thorpe, PA, and $11,520 per year over five years in Lower Burrell ([Times News](https://www.tnonline.com/?p=282076), [TribLive](https://mirror.triblive.com/local/valley-news-dispatch/artificial-intelligence-to-help-lower-burrell-public-works-save-money-plan-road-repairs)). HRM's incumbent budget line is a biennial high-speed survey, awarded to Englobe for **CA$225,002** in 2024 ([Bidsfactory](https://bidsfactory.com/en/tenders/high-speed-pavement-condition-data-collection-nova_scotia_tenders-038b9523f6aa3568)). HRM pressure is rising. Halifax saw roughly **twice as many potholes in 2026 as in 2025**, and a staff report proposed lowering the "Good" network target to 60% ([CTV](https://www.ctvnews.ca/atlantic/nova-scotia/article/potholes-have-almost-doubled-this-season-many-more-to-be-repaired/), [HRM council report](https://cdn.halifax.ca/media/85049)). The research found no AI road vendor deployed in Halifax or the Atlantic provinces. The only lead is a 2022 listing of Moncton as a CityROVER user ([IRCAI](https://ircai.org/top100/entry/sustainable-infrastructure-management-using-cityrover-ai/)). The province owns about 82% of Nova Scotia's road-km and is the larger buyer, but it was not studied ([CBC](https://amp.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.7369955)).

Against the market, RoadSense lacks several features buyers expect:

- **Segment-level condition scoring.** HRM plans capital work on a 0-100 PQI, and US practice uses PCI per ASTM D6433, which converts distress type, severity and extent into deduct values ([UC Davis CCPIC](https://www.ucprc.ucdavis.edu/ccpic/pdf/PCI%204-Pager%20final%20v2.pdf)).
- **Deterioration tracking over time**, which Samsara already offers.
- **Asset inventory**, such as lane markings and signs, which Vialytics already detects.
- **GIS export.**
- **French-language support**, which matters for expansion into New Brunswick and Quebec.

Image-to-PCI and image-to-PASER models exist, for example PaveCap's YOLOv8 + SAM single-shot PCI ([ROSAP](https://rosap.ntl.bts.gov/view/dot/94246/dot_94246_DS1.pdf)). A RoadSense condition score should be labelled as an estimate, not a certified survey. Repeated passes are the one thing an annual survey van cannot match. Dedupe already builds a time series per defect, so deterioration curves come almost for free.

For capture at scale, municipal fleets are the proven path. San José's pilot on city vehicles reported a **97% pothole true-positive rate**. Memphis runs cameras on four city trucks that also detect storm drains, high grass and dumping ([Cities Today](https://cities-today.com/?p=72693), [AP via Boston.com](https://www.boston.com/news/local-news/2025/11/16/cities-and-states-are-turning-to-ai-to-improve-road-safety)). Edge hardware is cheap: a $249 Jetson Orin Nano Super, or a Pi 5 with Hailo-8 at about $150-190 and 2.5 W, runs YOLOv8s at roughly 90+ FPS by one secondary benchmark ([CNX Software](https://cnx-software.com/2024/12/18/249-nvidia-jetson-orin-nano-super-developer-kit-targets-generative-ai-applications-at-the-edge/), [SpecPicks](https://specpicks.com/reviews/pi-5-hailo-8-vs-jetson-orin-nano-super-vision-2026)). RoadSense currently has no capture client at all. The "edge" is a laptop processing an uploaded file, and video-to-GPS alignment is a manual offset ([gps.py](../roadsense/edge/gps.py)).

## Prioritized roadmap: deliver, prove, then scale

The roadmap orders work by what unblocks a paid Halifax pilot first. Each later phase depends on the one before: a city cannot evaluate accuracy until reports reach its system, and fleet scale is worthless until the evidence survives a privacy review.

| **P0 — weeks** | Read-only HRM Cityworks SR/WO ingest from ArcGIS Hub, for dedupe against open requests and closed-loop status | Mock follow-up; no real outcome data | Public data, no IT approval needed; makes "time to repair" measurable | Low |
| **P0 — weeks** | Privacy hardening: authenticated, signed and expiring media URLs; server-side face and plate pass (EgoBlur) on stored crops; retention limits for uploaded video and GPS; rewrite SECURITY.md to match the web path | PIIDPA/FOIPOP exposure; unauthenticated evidence | Prerequisite for any PIA | Medium |
| **P1 — 1 quarter** | Canadian hosting plus a Canadian no-retention LLM/VLM, or a local Qwen2.5-VL-class verifier; Postgres/PostGIS, object storage, durable job queue, MCP over HTTP | Data residency; single-process, in-memory jobs | PIIDPA now, new FOIPOP in April 2027 | Medium-High |
| **P1** | Identity and RBAC (driver, fleet, city-staff roles; SSO via Entra ID); per-driver ownership of approvals; CI with pinned locks; metrics and alerting | Shared token; unauthenticated UI | Fails any municipal security review otherwise | Medium |

The commercial track runs in parallel. The entry route is a no-cost or low-value one-season HRM pilot, or an unsolicited proposal, on one or two city vehicles. After that comes annual SaaS plus a setup fee, priced against the US$5k-15k Vialytics anchor for small towns and the CA$225k survey line for HRM. Grant-funded validation could come through NRC IRAP, which already funded a $36,000 AI pavement pilot ([Open Canada grant](https://search.open.canada.ca/grants/record/nrc-cnrc%2C172-2024-2025-Q3-1024315%2Ccurrent)), or through Innovative Solutions Canada, whose calls list linear-infrastructure inspection as an area of interest ([ISC](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/innovative-solutions-canada/en/autonomous-systems-and-robotics-call-prototypes)). Several things remain unconfirmed:

- Which CRM, if any, sits in front of Cityworks at HRM.
- What NS Public Works' service-standard table and internal system look like.
- What HRM's procurement dollar thresholds are.
- Whether the new FOIPOP regulations allow any cross-border processing.

Each should be asked directly in the first conversation with HRM.

## Conclusion

The research changes RoadSense's identity more than its feature list. The MVP is framed as a citizen-dashcam 311 reporter, but the evidence points elsewhere. Crews find most potholes, Halifax's system of record is Cityworks, and liability makes unvetted detections a hazard for the city. The stronger product is an **inspection-to-work-order layer for Roads Operations**. Its human approval is a legal asset, not a UX cost, and its value grows as Samsara, Waze and Nexar flood cities with free, unverified detections. The guarded decision layer, with its hash-locked approval, evidence-gated verdicts and idempotent merges, is exactly what that position needs. It also explains why the cheapest wins are in the rules, not the models.

The binding constraints for a Halifax pilot are integration and data residency, not model accuracy. A RoadSense that files into Cityworks from Canadian infrastructure at 0.71 precision is pilotable. A RoadSense at 0.85 precision that writes JSON to a laptop is not. The pilot itself will produce Halifax ground truth through every approve and reject click, so accuracy work should follow deployment rather than precede it.
