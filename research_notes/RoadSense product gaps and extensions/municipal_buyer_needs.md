# Municipal and provincial road-authority buyer needs (Halifax / Nova Scotia / Canada)

Research date: 2026-11-12. Note: halifax.ca and cbc.ca pages returned HTTP 403 to the fetch tool, so several Halifax/CBC facts below come from search-result snippets of those pages, not full-page reads. They are flagged "(snippet)".

## 1. How HRM handles pothole reports today (channels, API, response times, volumes, HRM/Province split, pilots)

### Takeaway
HRM takes pothole reports through 311 (phone and a web form) and records them in Cityworks (Trimble), whose service requests and work orders are published on HRM's ArcGIS Hub open-data portal. No public Open311 GeoReport v2 endpoint for Halifax turned up. Provincial roads and 100-series highways belong to NS Public Works (511 or the Operations Contact Centre), not 311. Response targets are 7 to 30 business days by priority on HRM streets and 7 to 120 days on provincial roads. Volumes peak during the late-winter/spring freeze-thaw, and 2026 was roughly double 2025.

### Cited Findings
- HRM pothole page: residents report municipal-street potholes by calling 311 or using an online form. Provincial roads and 100-series highways go to 511 instead. Repairs take "7 business days to 30 business days depending on the pothole and if it is considered a priority." After a 311 call is logged, the business unit is notified, and staff typically connect within 10 business days. (snippet) — [HRM: Potholes and Sinkholes](https://www.halifax.ca/transportation/streets-sidewalks/paving-repair/report-a-pothole)
- News reports put priority-one potholes at up to 7 days and others at 30 days — [CTV Atlantic](https://atlantic.ctvnews.ca/we-ve-had-thousands-pothole-related-damages-on-the-rise-as-temperatures-grow-warmer-in-halifax-1.6831435); [CBC](https://amp.cbc.ca/news/canada/nova-scotia/pothole-problems-halifax-road-repair-disparity-1.4304148)
- HRM's open-data hub (ArcGIS Hub) publishes "Cityworks Service Requests" and "Cityworks Work Orders" datasets. Fields added in 2024-2025 include PROJECT_NAME, Work Order Cause and Work Order Resolution — [HRM Open Data notifications](https://data-hrm.hub.arcgis.com/pages/notifications-feedback)
- HRM datasets are exposed through GeoService (ArcGIS REST) and GeoJSON links plus an API Explorer, under the Open Government Licence. The program dates from Administrative Order 2014-006-ADM — [HRM Open Data FAQ](https://data-hrm.hub.arcgis.com/pages/open-data-common-questions)
- A separate HRM dataset covers 311 call volume (offered, handled and abandoned calls, plus talk time), updated daily — [HRM Open Data catalogue](https://data-hrm.hub.arcgis.com/datasets/open-data-catalogue-1)
- Volumes: CTV (April 9, 2026) reported Halifax dealing with "twice as many potholes this year compared to 2025" — [CTV](https://www.ctvnews.ca/atlantic/nova-scotia/article/potholes-have-almost-doubled-this-season-many-more-to-be-repaired/). Another CTV piece reported 5,100 potholes filled and 3,000 remaining — [CTV](https://www.ctvnews.ca/atlantic/nova-scotia/article/halifax-says-its-filled-5100-potholes-3000-remain-across-municipality/). In January 2025, HRM said it had "received about 560 work orders for potholes" and repaired about 260 (snippet, likely from) — [CTV: more potholes than 2024](https://www.ctvnews.ca/atlantic/nova-scotia/article/more-potholes-in-halifax-this-year-compared-to-2024-municipality-says/). Officials note that counts include duplicate reports of the same pothole — [seekyoursounds aggregator, low quality](https://www.seekyoursounds.com/news/halifax/halifax-sees-thousands-of-pothole-reports-in-early-2026)
- Province: NS Public Works gives itself "between seven and 120 days" to fix a pothole, depending on the hole's size and the road type. Reports go to the Operations Contact Centre (1-844-696-7737, dpw-occ@novascotia.ca) — [CBC, claims FOI](https://amp.cbc.ca/news/canada/nova-scotia/most-nova-scotia-pothole-compensation-claims-get-denied-1.7148599) (snippet); [The Laker](https://thelaker.ca/province-planning-to-fix-area-potholes-within-next-month/)
- The province owns more than 23,500 km of roads, about 82% of NS's network. Only 27% of NS roads were in good or very good condition in 2022 (StatCan) — [CBC](https://amp.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.7369955) (snippet)
- HRM pavement management: about 3,900 lane-km of arterial, collector and local streets. HRM moved to a Pavement Quality Index (PQI, 0 to 100) after 2016. A 2022 level-of-service report proposed a target of 67% of the network in "Good" condition and estimated $31.5M/yr to maintain the network average (~7.4 SDI). A 2024 staff report proposed lowering the target to at least 60%. The level of service was recommended by the 2019 Auditor General Road and Sidewalk Asset Management Audit. (snippets of HRM council reports on cdn.halifax.ca; the exact report URL mapping is not confirmed, and Council's final decision on the 60% target was not confirmed) — [HRM AG audit](https://hrmauditorgeneral.ca/themes/user/site/default/asset/img/common/Roads_and_Sidewalks_FINAL_Web_Print_Version_June_25.pdf); [HRM Paving & Repair](https://www.halifax.ca/transportation/streets-sidewalks/paving-repair)
- AI pilots in HRM: no HRM pothole or road-condition AI pilot found. HRM ran an AI camera wildfire-smoke pilot that ended when the vendor discontinued its service — [Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/11217065/halifax-residents-concerned-ai-wildfire-surveillance-program-ends). The province budgeted $1M (2026-27) for AI traffic signals in HRM — [Haligonia](https://haligonia.ca/?p=317074)

### Inferences
- The real integration target for HRM is Cityworks, either directly through the Cityworks REST API or through whatever 311 CRM sits in front of it, not an Open311 server. RoadSense's mock Open311 server does not represent HRM's actual stack. Any HRM integration will need IT approval and Cityworks API credentials.
- HRM's own open data (Cityworks SRs and WOs on ArcGIS REST) can be read for free today. That enables de-duplication against existing open requests and closed-loop status tracking without write access, which makes it a low-risk first integration.
- Because the jurisdiction split (HRM street vs provincial road vs 100-series highway) determines the channel and the SLA, RoadSense routing must use an authoritative road-ownership layer (HRM street centreline vs NS road network), not heuristics.
- The Pavement Quality Index (PQI) is HRM's capital-planning currency. RoadSense outputs aimed at capital planning should map to segment-level condition, not just point defects.

### Gaps
- No confirmation of which 311 CRM HRM uses in front of Cityworks (Salesforce, Dynamics, Cityworks PLL, or Cityworks alone). No confirmation of any HRM Open311 endpoint.
- HRM's priority-1 definition (size, depth, road class) was not found.
- No official HRM pothole counts by year, and no seasonal curve beyond news reports.
- NS Public Works' internal system (work-management / maintenance-management software) was not identified.

## 2. Work-order / asset-management / 311 CRM systems in Canadian municipalities and how they integrate

### Takeaway
The common Canadian pattern is a CRM intake layer (Salesforce at Toronto, others using Dynamics or SeeClickFix/CivicPlus) that dispatches to a GIS-centric work-order system (Cityworks on Esri ArcGIS is very common: Toronto, HRM, Barrie, Edmundston, Saint John Energy). Open311 GeoReport v2 exists but is an intake standard with real limits (one media_url, a binary open/closed status). Integrations to Cityworks typically go through its REST API or vendor connectors.

### Cited Findings
- Toronto: Salesforce is the 311 CRM. The Phase 1 pilot began December 2018, and Phase 2 (November 2021) covered about 650 services. Salesforce receives requests and dispatches them to on-premise systems including Cityworks, AMANDA and JDE — [Toronto staff report](https://www.toronto.ca/legdocs/mmis/2023/gg/bgrd/backgroundfile-238098.pdf) (snippet); [Salesforce case study](https://salesforce.com/resources/customer-stories/city-of-toronto). Toronto's 311 apps are "based on an open-standard (Open311) system" — (snippet, same search)
- Barrie, ON (Esri Canada, Dec 1, 2025): about 90% of potholes are found by Roads Department operators, who open Cityworks work orders. Citizen requests via Service Barrie are about 7%, and Road Patrol finds the rest. ArcGIS Collector handles high-volume repairs and syncs back to Cityworks. Each repair is tied to a road segment with a timestamp, which supports average-repair-time metrics — [Esri Canada](https://resources.esri.ca/cityworks/integrating-arcgis-and-cityworks-for-a-complete-pothole-management-solution)
- SeeClickFix (CivicPlus) sells a hosted connector to Cityworks AMS that needs Cityworks 15+ and a Citizen Engagement API licence. Cityworks 23.15.1 requires an "API Only" integration user, and MFA-enabled accounts are not supported — [CivicPlus: SeeClickFix Connect for Cityworks](https://www.civicplus.help/seeclickfix/docs/seeclickfix-connect-for-cityworks-requirements-configuration)
- Esri ArcGIS Workflow Manager can POST to the Cityworks API to create a service request — [Esri blog](https://www.esri.com/arcgis-blog/products/workflow-manager/developers/introducing-the-arcgis-workflow-manager-send-web-request-step). Esri Canada documents Cityworks integration strategies with external systems — [Esri Canada](https://resources.esri.ca/arcgis/integration-strategies-between-cityworks-and-external-systems-1-of-3)
- Infor's public-sector product ships an Open311 API with non-standard media and media_filepath parameters for attachments — [Infor docs](https://docs.infor.com/pub/11.2.x/en-us/pubolh/cmo1441041515897.html)
- Open311 GeoReport v2 spec: POST requires an API key, a service_code and one location (lat+long, address_string or address_id). attribute is required only if the service definition demands it. description is limited to 4,000 characters. media_url is a single optional URL with no upload, and "A convention for parsing media from this URL has yet to be established." status is only open or closed. status_notes, agency_responsible and expected_datetime are optional. The server may return a token instead of an ID. XML is required and JSON optional. GET defaults to 90 days or 1,000 records — [Open311 GeoReport v2](https://wiki.open311.org/GeoReport_v2)
- Live Open311 servers exist (e.g. Boston) — [Boston discovery](https://311.boston.gov/open311/v2/discovery.json); [Open311 server list](https://wiki.open311.org/GeoReport_v2/Servers)
- Kingston, ON integrates CITYROVER vehicle-camera pothole detections "into the City's GIS environment" — [CITYROVER case study (vendor)](https://www.cityrover.com/case-studies/city-of-kingston-on/)

### Inferences
- Barrie's 90/7 split suggests that most pothole work is crew-initiated, not citizen-reported. A dashcam product's main value may be as an inspection or road-patrol tool feeding work orders, not a "citizen 311" channel. That changes the target integration (work order or inspection record, not a service request) and the buyer (Roads Operations rather than 311 or Customer Service).
- The RoadSense Open311 adapter is worth keeping, but it needs companion adapters: Cityworks REST (SR, WO and inspection), ArcGIS Feature Service (push detections as a point layer with photos as attachments), and probably Salesforce or Dynamics.
- Open311's single media_url and binary status are insufficient for evidence (multiple frames, video clip, severity, dimensions). Those belong in attribute fields or the description, or in a separate evidence URL hosted in Canada.
- Agencies will expect duplicate suppression against existing open SRs, linear referencing (segment ID or street centreline ID), and status write-back.

### Gaps
- Current 311 CRMs for Ottawa, Calgary, Edmonton, Winnipeg and NS Public Works were not found.
- No Canadian market-share data for Cityworks, Cartegraph, Infor, Lagan/Verint or Dynamics.

## 3. Pavement condition standards (PCI/ASTM D6433, PASER, IRI) and capital vs reactive needs

### Takeaway
Capital planning runs on network-level, segment-based indices: PCI per ASTM D6433 in the US and many Canadian municipalities, HRM's own PQI/SDI, plus IRI roughness. Reactive maintenance runs on point defects that must be repaired within a statutory or policy window. A product must serve both: a point-defect work order with severity and dimensions, and segment-level distress type, severity and extent compatible with PCI-style deduct calculations.

### Cited Findings
- ASTM D6433 surveys record distress type, severity and extent for a sample unit. These convert to deduct values subtracted from 100 to give PCI — [UC Davis CCPIC PCI explainer](https://www.ucprc.ucdavis.edu/ccpic/pdf/PCI%204-Pager%20final%20v2.pdf)
- Under PCI, potholes are counted by low, medium and high severity, with severity tied to depth and diameter. The current edition referenced is D6433-24 — [TotalPave PCI asphalt distress training](https://totalpave.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/PCI-Asphalt-Distress-Training-20260707.pdf)
- Segments with the same PCI can have very different distress types and IRI, which is why roughness (IRI) is collected alongside PCI — [UC Davis CCPIC](https://www.ucprc.ucdavis.edu/ccpic/pdf/PCI%204-Pager%20final%20v2.pdf); [Wikipedia: PCI](https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavement_condition_index) (secondary)
- HRM uses a 0-100 PQI and SDI, with an LOS target of 60-67% "Good" — see section 1 (snippets)
- Durham Region and Richmond Hill (ON) use ROVER AI (a smartphone dash mount) for pothole detection. Richmond Hill's 135-day pilot fixed 542 potholes — [Durham Region](https://durham.ca/en/news/learn-about-how-durham-region-is-using-artificial-intelligence-to-optimize-road-maintenance.aspx); [Govlaunch: Richmond Hill](https://govlaunch.com/governments/richmond-hill-on/projects/richmond-hill-on-improves-pothole-identification-and-repair-time-with-rover-ai-technology)
- Gatineau (2024) put CityRover on a municipal truck. Size and location data were used to optimize repair routes — [QCNA](https://qcna.qc.ca/gatineau-turns-to-artificial-intelligence-in-municipal-modernization/). Victoria BC piloted AI on all 279 km of paved road — [CTV](https://vancouverisland.ctvnews.ca/victoria-using-ai-software-to-help-detect-potholes-1.6301188). Markham piloted a smartphone app — [CBC](https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/toronto/markham-pilots-ai-smartphone-app-to-detect-potholes-1.5591235)

### Inferences
- RoadSense currently detects "road damage" as point detections. To sell into capital planning, it would need a distress taxonomy aligned to D6433 (alligator cracking, longitudinal/transverse cracking, potholes, rutting, patching, raveling), severity levels, estimated extent per segment, and segment aggregation to a PCI-like index, all labelled as an estimate, not a certified survey.
- Repeated passes are a differentiator: deterioration over time (change detection) is something a single annual survey-vehicle pass cannot do.
- The competitive set in Canada is already established (ROVER/Visual Defence, CityRover, plus survey-vehicle vendors). Pilots happen at the municipal-roads-department level and run one season.

### Gaps
- PASER (Wisconsin 1-10 visual rating) adoption in Canada and IRI thresholds used by Canadian agencies were not found.
- No TAC (Transportation Association of Canada) or InfraGuide source was retrieved. NS Public Works' pavement index was not found.

## 4. Legal and regulatory: maintenance standards, liability, privacy, data residency, accessibility

### Takeaway
Liability turns on notice plus the service standard. In NS, the province pays only if it knew about a pothole and failed to fix it within its own standard, and most claims are denied. Ontario codifies this in O. Reg. 239/02 (size, depth and days by road class). A detection product therefore creates legal "notice," which is double-edged for the buyer. For privacy, NS public bodies (including municipalities) are governed by FOIPOP / MGA Part XX and PIIDPA, which currently bars storing or accessing personal information outside Canada. A new FOIPOP Act (Bill 150, Royal Assent Oct 3, 2025, in force April 1, 2027) repeals PIIDPA but keeps a cross-border restriction with exceptions set by regulation. NS's information-and-communication accessibility standard (WCAG) is still in development.

### Cited Findings
- NS provincial pothole claims: the department must have had prior notice (from the public or DPW patrols) and failed to meet its service standard. 2022: 935 claims, 105 paid. 2023: 374 claims, 64 paid (snippet). Denials can be reviewed by a manager, then an independent adjuster — [CBC FOI story](https://amp.cbc.ca/news/canada/nova-scotia/most-nova-scotia-pothole-compensation-claims-get-denied-1.7148599); [Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/10352240/ns-car-damage-potholes-claims)
- One claim was denied because the province's repair grace period hadn't elapsed — [Weather Network / CBC](https://www.theweathernetwork.com/en/news/lifestyle/travel/most-nova-scotia-claims-for-pothole-compensation-hit-a-dead-end-data-reveals)
- HRM: 309 pothole claims in 2013-14, 6 paid at reporting time (old data). Claims must be filed within 30 days via 311 or online (snippets, sources uncertain: [Global News 2014](https://globalnews.ca/news/1261174/harsh-halifax-winter-makes-for-costly-road-repairs/); [Signal HFX](https://signalhfx.ca/what-to-know-for-pothole-season/))
- Ontario O. Reg. 239/02 (Minimum Maintenance Standards): potholes must exceed both a surface-area and a depth threshold. For example, Table 1 lists 1,000 cm² and 8 cm, with 7, 14 and 30 days for Classes 3, 4 and 5 (per a municipal policy summary). Shorter windows apply to Class 1-2 roads, and Kitchener describes 4 days for heavy-traffic roads. Road class depends on speed and traffic. Meeting the MMS is a defence to liability — [e-Laws O. Reg. 239/02](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/020239); [Marmora and Lake LOS policy](https://marmoraandlake.ca/s/Level-of-Service-Policy.pdf); [Kitchener](https://www.kitchener.ca/potholes); [Mann Lawyers](https://www.mannlawyers.com/?p=1969). Verify current table values on e-Laws.
- NS privacy (current): PIIDPA s.5(1) requires that personal information in the custody of a public body, including municipalities, not be stored or accessed outside Canada, with limited exceptions. The head must report exceptions annually to the Minister — [OIPC NS MGA PIA](https://oipc.novascotia.ca/sites/default/files/publications/FINAL%20-%20MGA%20PIA%20OIPC%202019%2011%2029_0.pdf); [PIIDPA regulations](https://novascotia.ca/just/regulations/regs/piidpa.htm)
- New NS FOIPOP Act: it replaces FOIPOP, MGA Part XX and the Privacy Review Officer Act, and repeals PIIDPA. It adds mandatory breach notification for a "significant risk of harm," extends oversight to municipalities and villages, and takes effect April 1, 2027 — [NS news release, Sept 26, 2025](https://news.novascotia.ca/en/2025/09/26/province-introduces-modernized-access-privacy-legislation). Bill 150 received Royal Assent Oct 3, 2025. Disclosure outside Canada is restricted except "in accordance with regulations," which were not yet found — [NS Statutes 2025 Fall c.13](https://nslegislature.ca/sites/default/files/legc/PDFs/annual%20statutes/2025%20Fall/c013.pdf); [privacylawyer.ca](https://blog.privacylawyer.ca/2025/11/nova-scotias-new-freedom-of-information.html). The OIPC issued recommendations on the new Act (Aug/Sept 2026) — [OIPC NS](https://oipc.novascotia.ca/sites/default/files/2026%2008%2026%20Recommendations%20on%20the%20new%20FOIPOP%20Act.pdf)
- HRM has a corporate privacy policy — [HRM Privacy Policy](https://www.halifax.ca/city-hall/accountability-transparency/access-information-andor-records/privacy-policy)
- Accessibility: the NS Accessibility Act's Built Environment Standard was adopted March 2025. The Information and Communication standard is in development, with its final step targeted for 2028/29. The WCAG level is unconfirmed (vendor guesses: 2.1 AA) — [NS government accessibility plan](https://novascotia.ca/accessibility/plan/government-accessibility-plan.pdf); [Access by Design 2030](https://novascotia.ca/accessibility/access-by-design/); [Siteimprove (vendor)](https://siteimprove.com/en-ca/accessibility/nova-scotia-accessibility-act/)

### Inferences
- Dashcam frames capture faces, licence plates and sometimes building interiors, which is personal information. For any HRM or NS Public Works deployment, RoadSense should host imagery and processing in Canada (Canadian cloud region). LLM calls via OpenRouter to non-Canadian endpoints are a likely blocker if any personal information or images are sent. The product should blur faces and plates on-device or before upload, minimize retention, and support a Privacy Impact Assessment package. When the new FOIPOP takes effect (April 2027), the product needs breach-notification support and cross-border controls.
- Liability "notice" effect: a city that receives thousands of AI detections may be deemed to have notice of all of them, which starts its service-standard clock. Buyers will want detections to land as "inspection findings" triaged against MMS/HRM priority thresholds, with the human-approval step and an audit trail (who approved, when, evidence, measured size/depth), rather than as auto-filed citizen requests. This supports keeping the human-in-the-loop approval and adding severity estimates mapped to statutory thresholds and road class.
- Accessibility: plan for WCAG 2.1 AA (or 2.2 AA) on the operator UI. It is a typical RFP clause in Canada even before the NS standard is final.

### Gaps
- NS Public Works' actual pothole service-standard table (size and road class versus days) is not public in any form I found.
- No NS statute equivalent to Ontario's MMS was found. NS liability appears to be policy-based plus common-law negligence, but this was not confirmed with a legal source.
- The cross-border regulations under the new NS FOIPOP Act were not found. Their status as of Nov 2026 is unknown.
- No OPC/OIPC guidance specific to dashcam or vehicle-mounted imagery collected by or for municipalities was retrieved.

## 5. Procurement: how Canadian municipalities buy SaaS/AI, programs, budgets

### Takeaway
HRM buys under Administrative Order 2022-012-ADM, which is aligned with the NS Public Procurement Act and trade agreements (CFTA, CETA). Above low-value thresholds, that means competitive RFPs on public bid portals. Pilots typically come first: one season, one vehicle or a few vehicles, sometimes at no cost or low cost. Federal options include CCPI, which lets MASH-sector municipalities use PSPC instruments, including SaaS supply arrangements, if their province participates, and Innovative Solutions Canada (federal R&D contracts and testing, with no municipal-specific road challenge found).

### Cited Findings
- HRM Procurement Administrative Order 2022-012-ADM replaced 2020-004-ADM and is consistent with the NS Public Procurement Act, CFTA and CETA. A Procurement Manual sets out RFP, complex-quotation and major-tender procedures. HRM also has a Social Value Procurement framework — [HRM Procurement](https://www.halifax.ca/business/doing-business-halifax/procurement); [2020-004-ADM PDF](https://cdn.halifax.ca/sites/default/files/documents/business/doing-business-halifax/2020-004-ADM%20Procurement%20Policy.pdf); [Social Value FAQ](https://cdn.halifax.ca/sites/default/files/documents/business/doing-business-halifax/FAQ_Document_Social_Value_Procurement_October_2020.pdf)
- The NS provincial procurement policy recognizes unsolicited proposals ("not in response to a tender call") (2005 archived version) — [NS Procurement Policy 2005](https://bac-lac.wayback.archive-it.org/web/20061124103520/http://www.gov.ns.ca/tenders/policy/pdf_files/ProcurementPolicySeptember2005.pdf)
- CCPI: municipalities join through their province or territory. SaaS supply arrangements were opened to CCPI participants for the first time in 2023. Participants sign an access agreement and annually declare which instruments interest them — [PSPC CCPI how to join](https://www.canada.ca/en/public-services-procurement/services/acquisitions/collaborative-procurement-opportunities-governments-other-eligible-organizations/how-to-join.html); [CCPI commodity list](https://www.canada.ca/en/public-services-procurement/services/acquisitions/collaborative-procurement-opportunities-governments-other-eligible-organizations/list-commodities.html); [CCPI 2026 review](https://www.canada.ca/en/public-services-procurement/services/acquisitions/collaborative-procurement-opportunities-governments-other-eligible-organizations/reports-future-plans.html)
- Innovative Solutions Canada: the Testing Stream buys and tests pre-commercial innovations from Canadian companies through R&D contracts via PSPC calls for proposals. A Challenge Stream addresses specific federal needs. A call for prototypes lists "inspection of linear infrastructure (road...)" as an area of interest — [ISC Testing Stream roadmap](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/innovative-solutions-canada/en/node/107); [ISC autonomous systems call](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/innovative-solutions-canada/en/autonomous-systems-and-robotics-call-prototypes)
- Ontario's BPS innovation procurement primer describes when innovation procurement beats traditional procurement — [Ontario BPS Primer](https://www.doingbusiness.mgs.gov.on.ca/mbs/psb/psb.nsf/0/df7388300f40aec68525814d004a00bf/$FILE/BPS_Primer_on_Innovation_Procurement_Interim.pdf)
- Toronto awarded its 311 CRM implementation through RFP 3405-19-0301 (Catalyst Consulting) — [Toronto staff report](https://www.toronto.ca/legdocs/mmis/2020/ba/bgrd/backgroundfile-146746.pdf) (snippet)
- Pilot sizes seen in Canada: Richmond Hill ran a 135-day pilot; Kingston used 2 CITYROVER units in spring 2024; Gatineau used 1 truck in 2024 — sources in section 3

### Inferences
- The realistic HRM entry path is a low-value pilot under the sole-source or low-value thresholds, or a demo or unsolicited proposal, followed by a competitive RFP. Requirements to anticipate in that RFP: Canadian data residency, a PIA, SOC 2 or equivalent, WCAG 2.1 AA, Cityworks/ArcGIS integration, an export format (CSV, GeoJSON, shapefile), and single sign-on (Azure AD / Entra ID).
- NS Public Works purchases through the provincial procurement system (NS tenders portal). Because the province owns 82% of road-km, it is the larger addressable buyer in NS, but it was not studied here.
- ISC is an avenue to get a federal department to test the product, with funding. It is not a municipal channel. CCPI only matters if a SaaS supply arrangement the vendor is on covers this category.

### Gaps
- Current HRM dollar thresholds for low-value, quote and RFP were not retrieved (they are in AO 2022-012-ADM, which was not fetched).
- No typical budget or price points for municipal road-AI SaaS were found from reliable sources. Vendor pricing (ROVER, CityRover, Vialytics, RoadBotics) was not found. Per-km or per-vehicle-year pricing remains unknown.
- I found no evidence that NS participates in CCPI, and no list of SaaS supply-arrangement categories.
- FCM (Federation of Canadian Municipalities) asset-management program funding (MAMP) and the Canada Housing Infrastructure Fund were not researched as funding sources for pilots.
